What Does "Chotto Matte" Mean? ✋⏱️

"Wait a Minute! / Hold On!" — From Japanese Roots to Everyday Hawaii Pidgin

Quick Answer

"Chotto matte" (pronounced "CHOH-toh MAH-teh") means "Wait a minute!", "Hold on a sec!", or "Hang on!" Borrowed directly from Japanese (ちょっと待って: chotto = "a little bit" + matte = "wait"), it became a permanent fixture of Hawaii Pidgin during the plantation era and is still heard across Hawaii kitchens, offices, and beaches every day.

How to Pronounce "Chotto Matte"

CHOH-toh MAH-teh

Watch Out for One Classic Mainland Trap:

  • "Chot-to" (CHOH-toh) — rhymes with "photo," with a light crisp beat on the double t.
  • "Mat-te" (MAH-teh) — two syllables! The final -te is pronounced "teh" (never silent like English "matte finish").

Variations You'll Hear in Hawaii & Japanese

Phrase Tone & Meaning
Chotto matte! Casual & friendly: "Wait a sec!" / "Hold your horses!"
Chotto matte, brah! Pure local Hawaii Pidgin blend — Japanese phrase + Pidgin tag when someone is rushing out the door.
Chotto matte kudasai Polite Japanese: "Please wait a moment" — often said warmly by aunties, shopkeepers, and elders.
"Chotto!" (by itself) Quick shorthand for "Hey, wait a sec!" or "Just a tiny bit!"

Real Hawaii Pidgin Examples

"Chotto matte brah, I still tying my shoes!"

Translation: "Hold on a second bro, I'm still tying my shoes!"

"Chotto matte — lemme check if Aunty left her bag on da lānai."

Translation: "Wait a minute — let me check whether Aunty left her purse on the porch."

"Eh, chotto matte! No eat da musubi yet, I never take picture!"

Translation: "Hey, hold up! Don't eat the Spam musubi yet, I haven't taken a photo!"

Chotto Matte FAQ

What does "chotto matte" mean?

Chotto matte (pronounced CHOH-toh MAH-teh) means "Wait a minute!" or "Hold on a sec!" — from Japanese chotto ("a little") + matte ("wait").

Why do people in Hawaii say "chotto matte"?

Plantation-era Japanese immigrants helped shape modern Hawaii Pidgin, passing everyday expressions like chotto matte, bachi, shishi, and kimochi into island-wide speech.

What is the difference between "chotto matte" and "chotto matte kudasai"?

Chotto matte is casual among friends and family; adding kudasai ("please") makes it polite for customers, elders, or strangers.

How do you pronounce "chotto matte"?

Say CHOH-toh MAH-teh — make sure to pronounce the final -te as "teh," not like English "matte."

What is the Hawaii Pidgin equivalent of "chotto matte"?

"Try wait!" is the classic homegrown Pidgin counterpart meaning "Hold on a moment, please!"
