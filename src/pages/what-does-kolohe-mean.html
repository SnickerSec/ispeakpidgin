What Does "Kolohe" Mean? 😜🐕

Hawaii's Beloved Word for a Mischievous Rascal, Cheeky Prankster & Playful Troublemaker

Quick Answer

"Kolohe" (pronounced "koh-LOH-heh") is the Hawaiian and Pidgin word for mischievous, naughty, cheeky, or a lovable rascal. If a toddler sneaks an extra malasada, an uncle cracks a flirty joke at a backyard party, or a puppy steals your slippah off the lānai, every local in the room will laugh and say: "Ho, so kolohe!"

How to Pronounce "Kolohe"

koh-LOH-heh
  • "Ko-" (koh) — clean short "oh" vowel (never "kah-LOH-hee").
  • "-lo-" (LOH) — stressed middle syllable.
  • "-he" (heh) — don't drop the final vowel! Pronounce it "heh" (like "hen" without the n).

From Cute Rascal to Shady Behavior: Context Matters

Who You're Talking About What "Kolohe" Means
Keiki (Kids) & Pets 100% affectionate: spunky, curious, playful little rascal getting into harmless mischief.
Uncles, Friends & Flirting Cheeky, teasing, or slightly saucy humor — "Eh Uncle, no get kolohe!"
Serious Adult Business Crooked, sketchy, or unethical — "Dey caught him doing kolohe kine stuff wit' da contract."

Real Pidgin Sentence Examples

"Da keiki stay so kolohe — quiet in da kitchen means dey up to someting!"

Translation: "The kids are such little rascals — silence in the kitchen means they're up to mischief!"

"My dog one kolohe buggah, always digging puka under da fence."

Translation: "My dog is a mischievous little guy, always digging holes under the fence."

"She gave him da kolohe smile cuz she already ate da last manapua."

Translation: "She flashed him a cheeky grin because she had already eaten the last pork bun."

Kolohe Meaning FAQ

What does kolohe mean in Hawaiian?

Kolohe (pronounced koh-LOH-heh) means mischievous, rascal, naughty, or playful troublemaker.

Is "kolohe" an insult or a cute compliment?

Usually warm and affectionate for playful teasing of kids, friends, or pets — though in serious business contexts it can warn that someone is dishonest.

How do you pronounce kolohe?

Say koh-LOH-heh, pronouncing all three syllables clearly.

Why do so many dogs and cats in Hawaii have the name Kolohe?

Because it means "lovable little rascal" — making it one of the islands' all-time favorite pet names!

Can kolohe be used as a noun, adjective, and verb?

Yes — you can be kolohe, be one little kolohe, or tell someone "no go kolohe dat!"
