What Does "Kefe" Mean? 🛑

The Samoan Expletive Adopted Into Hawaii Street Slang

Quick Answer

"Kefe" (pronounced "KEH-feh") is a strong Samoan curse word and loanword in Hawaiian Pidgin meaning "jerk," "fool," "clown," or "punk." In native Samoan, it carries serious vulgarity (akin to "f***er" or "ass****"). In Hawaii local slang, it is used when someone acts foolish or recklessly cuts you off in traffic, but it is strictly taboo in polite company, family homes, or around church elders.

Important Cultural Etiquette & Severity

Unlike lighthearted teasing words like lolo (silly) or niele (nosy), kefe is genuine profanity.

  • Never say it to Samoan elders: In Samoan culture, using this word in front of parents, grandparents, or pastors is considered extremely disrespectful.
  • Never say it to strangers: Yelling "Eh kefe!" at someone you do not know is aggressive and frequently leads to physical fights.
  • Peer context only: You will only hear it used humorously between close friends ribbing each other for doing something dumb.

How to Pronounce "Kefe"

KEH-feh

Syllable Breakdown:

  • "Ke" - pronounced "keh" (short e like in "kettle" or "bed")
  • "fe" - pronounced "feh" (short e like in "fed" or "feather")

Key cadence: Emphasize the first syllable: KEH-feh. In local speech, it is delivered sharply and clipped.

Origins & Polynesian Connection

Hawaii is a multi-ethnic melting pot with deep ties across the Pacific. Beginning in the mid-20th century, substantial Samoan communities established roots across Hawaii—most notably in Oahu areas like Kalihi, Palolo, Waipahu, and the North Shore (Laie).

Through shared public schools, athletics, construction trades, and neighborhood life, Samoan vocabulary naturally crossed over into Hawaiian Pidgin. Words like kefe, palagi (foreigner/Caucasian), and musu (reluctant/grumpy) entered everyday island colloquialisms.

While Samoan speakers reserved kefe as one of their harsher expletives, Hawaii street slang softened its usage slightly among youth into a synonym for an annoying fool or clown, similar to how mainland urban slang adopts harsh terms for playful peer banter.

How Locals Use "Kefe" in Sentences

"Eh, kefe! Watch out, you goin' fall!"

Translation: "Hey, you fool! Pay attention, you're about to fall!"

"Da kefe buggah wen' cut me off on da freeway!"

Translation: "That jerk just cut me off on the freeway without signaling!"

"Why you acting like one kefe in front everybody?"

Translation: "Why are you acting like such a clown and embarrassing yourself in public?"
