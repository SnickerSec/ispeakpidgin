What Does "E Komo Mai" Mean? 🏡

Hawaii's Warmest Invitation: "Welcome — Come On Inside!"

Quick Answer

"E komo mai" (pronounced "eh KOH-moh MY") is the traditional Hawaiian phrase for "welcome," "come on in," or "please enter." You'll see it carved into wooden porch signs on island homes, painted across town welcome banners, and spoken warmly alongside Aloha whenever guests arrive at the door.

Word-by-Word Meaning & Pronunciation

eh KOH-moh MY
E ("eh")

Polite Hawaiian invitation / imperative particle ("please do...").

Komo ("KOH-moh")

Verb meaning "to enter, step inside, join in, or put on."

Mai ("MY")

Directional particle meaning "toward me / this way" (opposite of aku, "away").

Local House Rule: When a Hawaii host smiles and says "E komo mai!", kick off your shoes or rubbah slippahs on the lanai first — leaving a pile of slippahs by the front door is the #1 sign of a happy local gathering!

E Komo Mai Meaning FAQ

What does e komo mai mean in Hawaiian?

E komo mai (pronounced eh KOH-moh MY) means "welcome," "come in," or "please enter." Literally, e marks a polite invitation, komo means "enter," and mai means "toward the speaker."

How do you pronounce e komo mai?

Say eh KOH-moh MY — eh (like "bet"), KOH-moh (like "cocoa"), and MY (rhyming with "sky").

Does aloha mean welcome, or does e komo mai?

Aloha conveys love, hello, and goodbye, while e komo mai specifically means "welcome / come inside." Together, "Aloha, e komo mai!" means "Hello and welcome!"

What should you do when someone says "E komo mai" at a Hawaii home?

Slip off your shoes or flip-flops outside the front door before stepping inside — removing footwear at the entrance is universal respect in Hawaii homes.

How do you say "Welcome to our home" in Hawaiian?

Say "E komo mai i ko mākou hale" (or ko kākou hale when warmly including the guest in the household).
