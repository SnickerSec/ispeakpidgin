What Does "Fosho" Mean? 🤙

"For Sure," "Definitely" & "You Bet" in Slang and Island Talk

Quick Answer

"Fosho" (also written "fo sho" or "fo' sho'", pronounced "foh-SHOH") is casual slang for "for sure," "definitely," "absolutely," "you bet," or "no problem." While rooted in West Coast and African American Vernacular English (AAVE), fosho fits right into the non-rhotic (r-dropping) cadence of Hawaiian Pidgin, where locals pair it constantly with island staples like shoots, brah, and rajah.

How to Pronounce "Fosho"

foh-SHOH

Syllable Breakdown:

  • "fo" — pronounced "foh" (like "go" or "so," dropping the final "r" of "for")
  • "sho" — pronounced "SHOH" (like "show," with the main emphasis here)

Why it flows naturally in Hawaii: Hawaiian Pidgin naturally softens or drops post-vocalic "r" sounds (turning "brother" into "bruddah" and "over there" into "ova dea"), so "for sure" becomes a smooth, rhythmic foh-SHOH.

3 Ways People Use "Fosho"

1. Enthusiastic "Yes!"

Used to confirm plans or agree 100% without hesitation: "You going beach today?" — "Fosho!"

2. "Got It / Will Do"

Used to acknowledge a request or heads-up: "Grab ice on da way." — "Fosho, brah."

3. "No Problem / You're Welcome"

Used as a laid-back reply when someone thanks you: "Mahalo for da ride!" — "Fosho, anytime."

Fosho vs. Shoots vs. Rajah vs. Garanz

In Hawaii, locals have several favorite ways to say "yes," "for sure," and "sounds good." Here is how fosho compares with classic Pidgin affirmations:

Fosho (Fo Sho)

"For sure / Definitely." Confirms agreement, plans, or "no problem." Often paired: "Fosho, brah!"

Shoots

"Okay / Sounds good / See you later." Hawaii's #1 all-purpose green-light word for wrapping up plans.

Rajah (Rajah Dat)

"Roger that / Copy that / Understood." Confirms you heard the instructions loud and clear.

Garanz (Garanz Ballbaranz)

"100% Guaranteed." Stronger than fosho — used when swearing something is a total lock.

Real Sentence Examples in Hawaiian Pidgin

"You going surf Sandy's tomorrow morning?" — "Fosho, brah, see you 6 o'clock."

Translation: "Are you surfing Sandy Beach tomorrow morning?" — "For sure, bro, I'll see you at 6:00 AM."

"Shoots, fosho — just text me when you stay pau hana."

Translation: "Sounds good, definitely — just text me once you're done with work."

"Dat new poke spot get choke fish inside da bowl, fosho worth it."

Translation: "That new poke shop puts tons of fish in the bowl — it's definitely worth it."

"Mahalo for lending me your cooler, cuz!" — "Fosho, no worries!"

Translation: "Thanks for letting me borrow your cooler, friend!" — "Of course, no problem at all!"

Fosho FAQ

What does fosho mean?

Fosho (also spelled fo sho or fo' sho') is a phonetic slang contraction of "for sure." It means "definitely," "absolutely," "I agree," "you bet," or "no problem." In Hawaii, it is heavily used in everyday local talk alongside Pidgin staples like shoots, rajah, and brah.

How do you pronounce fosho?

It is pronounced foh-SHOH, dropping the "r" in both syllables with upbeat stress on the second syllable (SHOH).

Is fosho spelled one word or two ("fosho" vs "fo sho")?

Both spellings are widely used! In text messages and DMs, fosho as a single word is the fastest and most common spelling, while fo sho and fo' sho' are also popular when emphasizing the two-syllable rhythm.

How do locals in Hawaii use fosho vs shoots vs garanz?

In Hawaiian Pidgin, fosho means "definitely / for sure," shoots means "okay / sounds good / see you later," rajah means "got it / understood," and garanz means "100% guaranteed." Locals often stack them together: "Shoots, fosho brah!"
