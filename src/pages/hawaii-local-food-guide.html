The Story Behind Hawaii's Plate Lunch Culture

Hawaii's local cuisine was forged in the 19th-century sugar cane and pineapple plantation camps. Immigrant laborers from Native Hawaii, China, Japan, Portugal, the Philippines, Korea, and Puerto Rico shared their bento lunches in the fields, fusing ingredients into a vibrant, multi-ethnic comfort cuisine celebrated across the islands today.

Food in Hawaii is love and hospitality (aloha). Whether enjoying choke grindz at a family baby luau or picking up a hot plate lunch at Rainbow Drive-In, food language is an essential part of the island experience.

Island Classic

Loco moco

The Dish: Steaming white rice topped with a juicy hamburger patty, sunny-side-up fried egg, and rich brown gravy.

"Ho brah, I going get one loco moco with two eggs sunny side up!"

Dim Sum Fusion

Manapua

The Dish: Hawaii's version of Chinese char siu bao—a giant steamed or baked sweet yeast bun stuffed with sweet savory pork.

"Stop by Char Hung Sut and grab one dozen baked manapua for da boys."

Fresh Seafood

Poke bowl

The Dish: Cubed fresh raw ahi (yellowfin tuna) seasoned with shoyu, sesame oil, green onions, and limu, served over warm sushi rice.

"Spicy ahi poke bowl from Foodland always stay broke da mouth."

Iconic Snack

Spam musubi

The Dish: A slice of grilled teriyaki glazed Spam atop a block of seasoned rice, wrapped in crisp nori seaweed.

"Grab two warm Spam musubis for da beach trip!"

Sweet Treat

Malasada

The Dish: Portuguese fried dough balls rolled hot in sugar or cinnamon, often filled with haupia or lilikoi custard.

"Hot malasadas from Leonard's Bakery stay unbeatable."

Slang Phrase

Choke grindz

Meaning: A massive abundance of delicious food and feast provisions.

"Aunty wen make choke grindz for Uncle's retirement party."

Hawaii Food Slang You Need to Know

Broke da mouth

Extremely delicious food. The highest praise you can give a cook.

Kanak attack

The heavy post-meal food coma after eating a hearty plate lunch.

Kau kau

Traditional Pidgin word for food, eating, or mealtime.

Li hing mui

Salted dried plum powder dusted over gummy candies, shave ice, and pineapple.

How to Order at a Local Hawaii Drive-In

When pulling up to the counter at iconic drive-ins like Rainbow Drive-In, L&L, or Keneke's, keep your order clear, polite, and local:

  • "Two scoops rice, one scoop mac salad" — The universal plate lunch default. If you prefer salad or extra rice, specify right away.
  • "Gravy all ova" — Requesting rich brown gravy poured generously across both the hamburger patty and rice scoops.
  • "Mini plate" vs "Regular plate" — Mini plates come with 1 scoop of rice and slightly smaller portions for lighter appetites.
