What Does "Lānai" (Lanai) Mean? 🌺🏡

Hawaiian for Porch, Veranda & Balcony — Plus How It Differs from Lānaʻi Island

Quick Answer

"Lānai" (often written lanai and pronounced "lah-NAI") is the Hawaiian word for a roofed porch, veranda, covered patio, or balcony. In Hawaii, nobody says "step out onto the balcony" — whether it's a plantation house veranda in Maui or a 25th-floor hotel balcony in Waikīkī, locals call it "da lānai."

Crucial Distinction: Lānai (Porch) vs. Lānaʻi (Island)

When diacritical marks are stripped in English signs, these two words look identical (lanai) — but in Hawaiian they are pronounced completely differently because of a single ʻokina (glottal stop):

Hawaiian Word Pronunciation Meaning
Lānai (no ʻokina) lah-NAI / LAH-nai (2 syllables) Covered porch, veranda, deck, patio, or condo/hotel balcony (historically a shaded bower or pavilion).
Lānaʻi (with ʻokina) lah-NAH-ee (3 syllables) Hawaii's sixth-largest island ("The Pineapple Island"), located across the ʻAuʻau Channel from Maui.

Everyday Island Examples

"Eh brah, come sit on da lānai — get nice trade winds an' cold pūpū!"

Translation: "Hey bro, come sit out on the porch — there's a great breeze and cold appetizers!"

"Leave your rubbah slippahs outside on da lānai before you come in."

Translation: "Slip off your flip-flops out on the front porch before coming inside the house."

"No hang beach towels ova da condo lānai railing, da AOAO stay strict!"

Translation: "Don't drape beach towels over the apartment balcony railing, the condo association is strict!"

Lānai Meaning FAQ

What does lānai (lanai) mean in Hawaii?

Lānai (pronounced lah-NAI) means a covered porch, veranda, patio, or apartment/hotel balcony.

What is the difference between a lānai (porch) and the island of Lānaʻi?

Lānai (porch) has no ʻokina and two syllables (lah-NAI). Lānaʻi (the island) has an ʻokina before the final i and three syllables (lah-NAH-ee).

How is a lānai different from a regular porch or patio?

A lānai is overhead-roofed to block tropical sun and passing rain showers while staying open to island breezes, acting as an outdoor living room.

Why do people in Florida also say "lanai"?

Mid-century builders adopted the Hawaiian architectural term for covered, screened-in pool patios across Florida and Sunbelt homes.

Do you take your shoes off on a lānai in Hawaii?

Yes — at a house, shoes and slippahs stay lined up on the front lānai before entering; on a condo balcony, you step out barefoot from inside.
