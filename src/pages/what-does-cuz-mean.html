What Does "Cuz" Mean in Hawaii? 🤙

Pidgin for Cousin, Close Friend & Extended Island ʻOhana

Quick Answer

In Hawaiian Pidgin, "cuz" (pronounced "kuhz", short for cousin, and affectionately called "cuzzie") means "cousin," "bro," "close friend," or "buddy." Whether someone greets you with "Howzit, cuz!" at the beach or says "Mahalo, cuz" at a lunch wagon, calling someone cuz welcomes them into Hawaii's extended ohana (family) circle. Note: in fast Pidgin texting, cuz can also be shorthand for "because" ("No can go cuz get work") — context makes it instant to tell apart!

How to Pronounce "Cuz" & "Cuzzie"

kuhz  /  KUH-zee
  • "Cuz" (kuhz) — Rhymes with "buzz" and "was." Short, warm, and upbeat.
  • "Cuzzie" (KUH-zee) — Two-syllable affectionate form popular across Hawaii and the wider Polynesian Pacific.

Tone matters: With a warm smile or head nod ("Howzit, cuz!"), it means "my friend / family." Said sharply with a drop in tone ("Eh, cuz..."), it means "Hold on a second, watch yourself."

Why Everyone in Hawaii Is "Cuz," "Aunty," or "Uncle"

One of the first things visitors notice in Hawaii is that locals rarely address people with stiff titles like "Sir" or "Ma'am." Instead, island speech is built around kinship terms rooted in ohana (extended family):

Peers & Friends Cuz, Cuzzie, Brah, Bruddah, Sistah, Tita
Elders & Older Generation Aunty, Uncle, Tūtū (used out of respect even if not blood-related)

Also, in tight-knit island communities, people genuinely have choke (lots of) first, second, and third cousins — plus "calabash cousins" (families so close they grew up sharing the same calabash bowl of poi). Calling someone cuz bridges that bond immediately.

Cuz vs. Brah vs. Bruddah

How do locals choose between cuz, brah, and bruddah? Here is the breakdown:

Cuz / Cuzzie

Family-vibe & tight bond. Used for actual cousins, lifelong friends, or warm neighborhood camaraderie ("Thanks, cuz!").

Brah

Universal casual address. The everyday go-to in surf lineups, traffic, and quick conversation ("Eh brah, how da waves?").

Bruddah

Heartfelt brotherhood. Warmer and more personal, often used as a noun or title of affection ("Dat's one solid bruddah").

Real Sentence Examples in Hawaiian Pidgin

"Howzit, cuz! Long time no see — how da fambly?"

Translation: "Hey cousin/friend! It's been a while — how is your family doing?"

"Mahalo for da plate lunch, cuz — broke da mouth!"

Translation: "Thanks so much for the plate lunch, bro — it was unbelievably delicious!"

"Dat's my cuzzie from Waianae — we grew up surfing together."

Translation: "That's my cousin (or lifelong friend) from Waianae — we grew up surfing together."

"Shoots, cuz, I catch you bumbai after work!"

Translation: "Sounds good, friend, I'll catch up with you later after work!"

Cuz Meaning FAQ

What does "cuz" mean in Hawaii?

In Hawaiian Pidgin, cuz (short for cousin, also affectionately called cuzzie) means a biological cousin, a close friend, a peer, or a friendly stranger. Just like brah, bruddah, sistah, aunty, and uncle, calling someone cuz reflects Hawaii's extended ohana (family) culture.

Why do people in Hawaii call everyone "cuz" or "cousin"?

In Hawaiian and local island culture, ohana (family) extends far beyond blood relatives. Addressing a friend, coworker, or neighbor as cuz (and elders as Aunty or Uncle) immediately treats them with the warmth, trust, and mutual respect of family.

What is the difference between cuz, brah, and bruddah?

All three mean friend or bro in Hawaii, with subtle nuances: brah is the most universal casual address (like "bro" or "dude"), bruddah feels slightly warmer and more soulful ("my bruddah"), and cuz emphasizes a tight, family-style bond ("howzit, cuz!").

What does "cuzzie" mean?

Cuzzie (pronounced KUH-zee) is an affectionate variation of cuz used across Hawaii and Polynesia (especially Samoa, Tonga, and Aotearoa New Zealand) to refer to a cousin or tight friend.
