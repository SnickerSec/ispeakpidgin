What Does "Mahalo Nui Loa" Mean? 🌺

Hawaii's Most Heartfelt Phrase for "Thank You Very Much"

Quick Answer

"Mahalo nui loa" (pronounced "mah-HAH-loh NOO-ee LOH-ah") is the Hawaiian phrase for "thank you very much" or "thank you so very much." Built from three Hawaiian words — mahalo (thanks, gratitude), nui (big, great), and loa (very, extremely) — it is the warmest, most complete expression of deep appreciation in Hawaii. You will also frequently hear the slightly shorter "mahalo nui" ("big thanks" or "thank you so much") in everyday island conversation.

How to Pronounce "Mahalo Nui Loa"

mah-HAH-loh NOO-ee LOH-ah

Word & Syllable Breakdown:

  • "Mahalo" (mah-HAH-loh) — Three syllables with stress on the middle HAH (short "ah" like "father," never "hay").
  • "Nui" (NOO-ee) — Two pure vowels that glide smoothly together: "noo" (like "moon") + "ee" (like "see").
  • "Loa" (LOH-ah) — "Loh" (like "low") followed by a soft "ah."

Tip: Let the vowels flow cleanly without hard English consonants at the ends. Practice the cadence as three gentle waves: mah-HAH-loh · NOO-ee · LOH-ah.

Word-by-Word Hawaiian Breakdown

In ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi (the Hawaiian language), modifiers follow the word they describe, stacking to build intensity:

1. Mahalo Thanks, gratitude, admiration, praise, esteem
2. Nui Big, large, great, abundant, important
3. Loa Very, long, far, exceedingly, supreme

When you say mahalo nui, you are offering "great thanks." Adding loa stretches that gratitude as far as it can go — literally "thanks great and far-reaching." Locals save mahalo nui loa for moments when a simple "thanks" isn't enough: when someone goes out of their way to help your family, hosts you generously, or shares meaningful kokua (help and care).

Mahalo vs. Mahalo Nui vs. Mahalo Nui Loa vs. Mahalo Plenty

In Hawaii, people switch naturally between traditional Hawaiian gratitude phrases and casual Hawaiian Pidgin depending on the moment:

Phrase Literal Meaning Vibe & Best When to Use
Mahalo "Thank you" Universal everyday thanks — coffee shops, holding a door, quick favors.
Mahalo nui "Big thanks / Thank you so much" Warm, conversational extra thanks to friends, coworkers, and neighbors.
Mahalo nui loa "Thank you very much" Deepest, most respectful gratitude — speeches, cards, major acts of kindness.
Mahalo plenty "Thanks a lot" (Pidgin) Friendly local Hawaiian Pidgin blend when thanking a friend or neighbor casually.

Real Sentence Examples in Hawaii

"Mahalo nui loa for watching da keiki yesterday, Aunty!"

Translation: "Thank you so very much for babysitting the kids yesterday, Aunty!"

"Mahalo nui for da mango from your yard — stay so ono!"

Translation: "Thank you so much for the mangoes from your tree — they are delicious!"

"Mahalo nui loa for your kokua keeping our beach clean."

Translation: "Thank you very much for your help and care in keeping our beach clean."

"Mahalo plenty for helping me move da truck, brah — mahalo nui loa!"

Translation: "Thanks a ton for helping me load the truck, bro — I really appreciate it!"

How to Respond to "Mahalo Nui Loa"

When someone tells you "Mahalo nui loa," here are the most natural ways to say "You're very welcome" in Hawaii:

"ʻAʻole pilikia"

Pronounced ah-OH-leh pee-lee-KEE-ah — Hawaiian for "No trouble at all" (the classic Hawaiian reply).

"No worries!" / "No worry, beef curry!"

Warm, everyday local response meaning "You're welcome, happy to help."

"Shoots!" / "Rajah!"

Casual Pidgin acknowledgment meaning "Anytime!" or "You got it!"

"Aloha!"

Returned with a warm smile or shaka to share mutual appreciation.

Mahalo Nui Loa FAQ

What does mahalo nui loa mean in Hawaiian?

Mahalo nui loa (pronounced mah-HAH-loh NOO-ee LOH-ah) means "thank you very much" or "thank you so very much" in Hawaiian. It combines mahalo (thanks/gratitude), nui (big/great), and loa (very/long/far) to express deep, heartfelt appreciation.

How do you pronounce mahalo nui loa?

It is pronounced mah-HAH-loh NOO-ee LOH-ah. Mahalo puts stress on the middle syllable (HAH), nui glides from "NOO" to "ee," and loa is pronounced "LOH-ah."

What is the difference between mahalo nui and mahalo nui loa?

Both mean "thank you very much," with a slight difference in emphasis. Mahalo nui literally means "big thanks" and is very common in warm everyday conversation, while mahalo nui loa adds loa ("very/exceedingly") for the fullest, most formal or heartfelt gratitude.

How do you respond to mahalo nui loa?

In Hawaiian, reply with ʻAʻole pilikia (ah-OH-leh pee-lee-KEE-ah), meaning "no trouble at all." In everyday Hawaii English and Pidgin, locals reply with "No worries," "Shoots," "Anytime," or "Aloha!"

What is the Hawaiian Pidgin equivalent of mahalo nui loa?

Locals use mahalo nui loa across Hawaiian, Pidgin, and English alike, and also say "mahalo plenty" in casual Hawaiian Pidgin to mean "thanks a lot!"
