What Does "Holoholo" (Holo Holo) Mean? 🛻🌊

Hawaii's Favorite Word for Cruising Around, Exploring & Getting Out of the House

Quick Answer

"Holoholo" (also written holo holo or holo-holo, pronounced "HOH-loh HOH-loh") means to go out for pleasure — cruising around the island, taking a walk, going for a boat ride, or exploring with no strict destination or rush. It comes from reduplicating the Hawaiian root verb holo ("to run, ride, sail, or travel").

Pronunciation: Holo vs. Holoholo

HOH-loh HOH-loh

How Hawaiian Reduplication Changes the Vibe:

  • Holo (HOH-loh) — Hawaiian verb meaning to run, sprint, ride, or sail swiftly (e.g., "Da dog wen' holo fast!" or "Holo, holo — we gotta go!").
  • Holoholo (HOH-loh HOH-loh) — repeating holo softens purposeful speed into relaxed, joyful motion: going out and about just to enjoy the breeze, scenery, and company.
  • No ʻokina or kahakō needed — every o is a clean, rounded "oh" sound.

4 Classic Ways Locals Use Holoholo

Expression What It Means in Local Life
"We go holoholo!" Weekend call to hop in the truck, roll the windows down, cruise the coast, and stop wherever looks good.
Holoholo Fishing Dragging a line casually behind a boat, canoe, or kayak while cruising — out for the ocean first, grindz second.

"Holo to da store" Single holo = a quick, purposeful dash or errand ("I going holo Longs real quick").

Real Pidgin Sentence Examples

"Eh, nice day out — you guys like go holoholo up North Shore?"

Translation: "Hey, it's beautiful outside — do you all want to take a scenic drive up to the North Shore?"

"No real plan today, me an' Tutu stayed holoholo around town an' talk story."

Translation: "We didn't have a set schedule today; Grandma and I just cruised around town chatting."

"Every time I grab my slippahs, da dog tink we going holoholo!"

Translation: "Every time I put on my flip-flops, the dog thinks we're going out for an adventure!"

"Holo, holo — da bell stay ringing already!"

Translation: "Run, hurry up — the school bell is already ringing!"

Holoholo Meaning FAQ

What does holoholo (or holo holo) mean in Hawaii?

Holoholo (pronounced HOH-loh HOH-loh) means going out for pleasure — taking a scenic drive, strolling, boating, or exploring without a rigid itinerary.

What does the root word "holo" mean in Hawaiian?

Single holo means "to run, ride, sail, or travel." Reduplicating it to holoholo turns fast, directional movement into leisurely cruising.

What does "holoholo fishing" mean?

Trailing a fishing lure casually while boating, paddling, or kayaking from place to place — enjoying the water first rather than grinding hard for a catch.

Is it spelled "holoholo", "holo holo", or "holo-holo"?

Standard Hawaiian spells it as one word: holoholo. In everyday Hawaii Pidgin, holo holo and holo-holo are equally common.

How do locals use "we go holoholo" in a sentence?

Say "We go holoholo!" anytime you want to invite family, friends, or even your dog out of the house for an unhurried island adventure.
