What Does "Huhu" Mean? 😤

Hawaiian & Pidgin for "Angry, Mad & Upset" — Why You Never Want Aunty Stay Huhu!

Quick Answer

"Huhu" (written "huhū" in Hawaiian and pronounced "hoo-HOO") means "angry," "mad," "upset," or "offended." Borrowed straight from ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi into everyday Hawaii Pidgin, it covers everything from playful teasing ("Eh, no get huhu!") to a serious warning when you forgot to take your slippahs off at the front door ("Ho, Mom stay real huhu!").

How to Pronounce "Huhu" (Huhū)

hoo-HOO

Syllable Breakdown:

  • "hu" (hoo) — rhymes with "who" or "too" (never "huh-huh" like English "uh-huh").
  • "hū" (HOO) — in Hawaiian, the kahakō macron over the second ū stretches the final "oo" slightly longer.

Literal Root: In Hawaiian imagery, huhū also relates to swelling up or overflowing — picture anger rising like boiling water or ocean surge!

Real Sentence Examples in Hawaii

"Eh, no make me huhu — go clean your room right now!"

Translation: "Hey, don't make me angry — go clean your room right now!"

"Why you stay so huhu, brah? I was only joking!"

Translation: "Why are you so mad, bro? I was just teasing!"

"She get real huhu when somebody eat da last manapua without asking."

Translation: "She gets genuinely upset when someone eats the last pork bun without asking."

"No go sleep huhu — talk story first and make tings pono."

Translation: "Don't go to bed angry — talk it out first and make things right."

Huhu Meaning FAQ

What does huhu mean in Hawaiian and Pidgin?

Huhu (spelled huhū in Hawaiian) means "angry," "mad," "upset," "offended," or "irritated." Locals across Hawaii use it daily in Pidgin sentences like "No make me huhu!" ("Don't make me mad!").

How do you pronounce huhu?

It is pronounced hoo-HOO (rhyming with "too"), never "huh-huh." In formal Hawaiian (huhū), the kahakō macron lengthens the second syllable slightly.

What does "mai huhū" mean?

"Mai huhū" is the traditional Hawaiian phrase for "don't be angry" or "please don't take offense." In everyday Hawaii Pidgin, locals say "No get huhu!" or "No stay huhu wit me!"

What is the difference between huhu and humbug?

Huhu is the emotion of feeling angry or mad at someone, while humbug describes an annoying hassle or inconvenient situation.

How do locals resolve things when someone is huhu?

In Hawaiian family life, lingering anger (huhū) is traditionally healed through hoʻoponopono — talking things through honestly with apology and forgiveness until everyone's heart is pono (right) again.
