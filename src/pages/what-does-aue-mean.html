What Does "Auē / Auwē" Mean? 😱🌺

Hawaii's Classic Exclamation for "Oh No!", "Oh Dear!", Sympathy & Shock

Quick Answer

"Auē!" (often spelled auwē, aue, or auwe, and pronounced "ah-WEH" or "ow-WEH") is the classic Hawaiian and Pidgin exclamation for "Oh no!", "Oh my gosh!", "Oh dear!", or "Alas!" Depending on voice tone, it can express sudden surprise, auntie-style scolding, or deep heartfelt sympathy.

Why You See Both "Auē" and "Auwē"

ow-WEH / ah-WEH
  • Natural "w" glide: Moving smoothly from au to long ē naturally produces a "w" sound in the mouth.
  • Modern Hawaiian spelling (auē): Leaves the predictable glide unwritten and marks the held final vowel with a kahakō (ē).
  • Pidgin & classic spelling (auwē / auwe): Writes the "w" right on the page just the way Tutu says it out loud!

4 Everyday Moods of "Auē!"

How It Sounds What the Speaker Feels
Quick, sharp "Auwe!" Sudden oops or mild shock — forgetting your wallet at home or dropping a plate of poke.
Hands-on-hips "Auwe, you kolohe!" Affectionate local exasperation when kids or pets track mud across the floor.
Soft, drawn-out "Auuuē..." Genuine compassion, pity, or shared grief ("Oh dear, I'm so sorry to hear that").
"Auē nohoʻi ē!" (Hawaiian) Heightened poetic exclamation: "Goodness gracious! / What a sight!"

Real Pidgin Sentence Examples

"Auē, I forgot my wallet on da kitchen counter!"

Translation: "Oh no, I left my wallet back on the kitchen counter!"

"Auwe! Look da mess da puppy wen' make in da yard!"

Translation: "Oh my gosh! Look at the mess the puppy made in the yard!"

"Auē, da H-1 traffic stay jam up all da way past Pearl City."

Translation: "Ugh, oh man, freeway traffic is backed up all the way past Pearl City."

Auē / Auwē FAQ

What does auē (or auwe) mean in Hawaiian and Pidgin?

Auē (pronounced ah-WEH or ow-WEH) means "Oh no!", "Oh dear!", "Goodness!", or "Alas!" — expressing surprise, sympathy, or exasperation.

Why is it spelled both "auē" and "auwē"?

Modern Hawaiian writes auē (since the w glide between u and ē is automatic), while older Hawaiian texts and Pidgin spell out the spoken glide as auwē or auwe.

How does tone change the meaning of "auē!"?

Short and snappy means "Oops / Oh snap!"; soft and drawn out means compassionate "Oh dear, how sad."

Is "auē" shared across Polynesia?

Yes — it is a shared Polynesian interjection heard across Hawaiian, Māori, Samoan, and Tahitian.

How do you pronounce auē / auwe?

Say ow-WEH or ah-WEH, stressing and slightly lengthening the final "weh" syllable.
