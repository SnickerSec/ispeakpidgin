What Does "Kahuna" Mean? 🌿

Hawaiian for Revered Master Expert, Healer & Keeper of Sacred Knowledge

Quick Answer

"Kahuna" (pronounced "kah-HOO-nah") is a deeply respected Hawaiian title meaning a master expert, traditional healer, skilled craftsperson, navigator, or spiritual priest. While mainland pop culture turned "the big kahuna" into casual slang for a boss or top dog, in Hawaii a kahuna is a lifelong practitioner who carries sacred ancestral mastery (ʻike) in a dedicated field.

Pronunciation & The "Cahunas / Cojones" Mix-Up

kah-HOO-nah

Syllable Breakdown:

  • "ka" (kah) — open "ah" sound like "father".
  • "HU" (HOO) — primary stress, like "who" or "moon".
  • "na" (nah) — smooth "nah" ending (plural in Hawaiian is nā kahuna or kāhuna — never "kahunas").

Common Mainland Mix-Up ("Cahunas" / "Kahoonas"): People searching "cahunas" or "kahoonas" are often either spelling kahuna phonetically OR confusing Hawaiian kahuna (kah-HOO-nah, master expert) with Spanish cojones (koh-HOH-ness, slang for nerve/guts). They come from completely different languages!

Traditional Fields of Hawaiian Kāhuna

In ancient and modern Hawaiian society, a kahuna is never a vague generalist — the title is paired with the exact craft or healing art they mastered:

Hawaiian Title Domain of Mastery
Kahuna Lāʻau Lapaʻau Master of traditional Hawaiian herbal medicine and plant healing.
Kahuna Lomilomi Master practitioner of restorative Hawaiian therapeutic massage and bodywork.
Kahuna Kālai Waʻa Master voyaging canoe architect and timber selector — selecting sacred koa trees in the forest.
Kahuna Kilo Hōkū Master astronomer and star navigator reading swells, winds, and constellations.
Kahuna Pule Ceremonial priest and keeper of chants, protocol, and temple (heiau) prayers.

Island Etiquette: How Locals Use (and Don't Use) Kahuna

Respectful Usage

Referring to recognized Hawaiian cultural practitioners, healers, or elders with deep respect — or occasionally praising someone's lifelong craft in warm Pidgin ("Uncle stay one real kahuna on da surfboard shaper").

What to Avoid in Hawaii

Never put "Kahuna" in your LinkedIn job title ("Marketing Kahuna") or call yourself a "Big Kahuna" in Hawaii. Using a sacred Hawaiian title for corporate branding feels cringey and disrespectful to locals. Use akamai ("smart/skilled") instead!

Real Sentence Examples in Hawaii

"Da kahuna bless da new waʻa before we launch from Makapuʻu."

Translation: "The Hawaiian priest/elder blessed the new outrigger canoe before we launched from Makapuʻu."

"Her tutu went see one kahuna lāʻau lapaʻau up Waimea side."

Translation: "Her grandmother visited a traditional Hawaiian herbal healer up near Waimea."

"He one kahuna in da art of surfing — forty years shaping boards on da North Shore."

Translation: "He's a true master in the art of surfing — forty years shaping boards on the North Shore."

"No call yourself kahuna, brah — let your work talk fo' you."

Translation: "Don't call yourself a master, bro — let your actions speak for themselves."

Kahuna Meaning FAQ

What does kahuna mean in Hawaiian?

In Hawaiian (ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi), kahuna (pronounced kah-HOO-nah) means a master expert, licensed specialist, traditional healer, or priest who holds deep ancestral knowledge in a specific discipline — such as herbal medicine (kahuna lāʻau lapaʻau), canoe carving (kahuna kālai waʻa), or celestial navigation.

Where did the slang phrase "Big Kahuna" come from?

"Big Kahuna" grew out of 1950s–1960s California surf culture (popularized by the movie Gidget, where "The Great Kahoona" was a surfer's nickname). Mainland English adopted it to mean "the boss" or "most important person," whereas in Hawaii kahuna remains a respected traditional title.

Is "cahunas" or "kahoonas" the same word as kahuna?

People often spell kahuna phonetically as "kahoonas" or "cahunas," and mainland speakers frequently mix up Hawaiian kahuna (master expert, kah-HOO-nah) with Spanish cojones (slang for nerve/guts, koh-HOH-ness). The two words are completely unrelated.

How do you pronounce kahuna?

It is pronounced kah-HOO-nah, with stress on the middle syllable (HOO) and pure Hawaiian vowels.

Is it respectful to call yourself a kahuna in Hawaii?

No — calling yourself a kahuna (or using "Kahuna" on business cards or marketing) is seen as culturally tone-deaf in Hawaii because authentic kāhuna train for decades under elders.
