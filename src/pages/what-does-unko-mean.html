What Does "Unko" Mean in Hawaii? 💩🤙

Why Pronunciation Matters: "OON-koh" (Poop) vs. "UN-koh" (Uncle)

Quick Answer

If you see or hear "unko" in Hawaii, pay close attention to the vowel sound — because one spelling covers two completely different local words:

1. unko (pronounced "OON-koh") — The everyday Hawaiian Pidgin word for "poop" (borrowed from Japanese うんこ / unko), heard whenever someone steps in dog poop on the lawn or changes a baby's diaper ("Watch out fo' da dog unko!").
2. Unko / Unco (pronounced "UN-koh") — A casual phonetic spelling of "Uncle", Hawaii's warm title of respect for any older man, elder, or family friend ("Eh, Unko, howzit!").

Crucial Pronunciation Guide: Don't Mix Them Up!

Meaning #1: Poop 💩 OON-koh
  • "OON" — Sounds like "moon" or "spoon" (Japanese u vowel).
  • "koh" — Crisp "koh" sound like "cocoa."
  • Origin: Japanese うんこ (unko), used by Hawaii families of all backgrounds.
Meaning #2: Uncle 🤙 UN-koh
  • "UN" — Short "uh" sound just like English "under" or "uncle."
  • "koh" — Relaxed final syllable of Pidgin "Uncle."
  • Spelling Tip: Many locals spell Uncle as "Unco" or "Uncle" in texts specifically so nobody reads it as OON-koh!

Unko Meaning FAQ

What does unko mean in Hawaii?

In Hawaii, unko can mean two completely different things depending on how it is pronounced: (1) Pronounced OON-koh (from Japanese うんこ unko), it is the everyday Hawaiian Pidgin word for "poop" or "dog poop" ("Watch out for da dog unko!"). (2) Pronounced UN-koh, it is a playful phonetic spelling of "Uncle" — Hawaii's respectful and affectionate title for any older man.

How do you tell the difference between unko (poop) and Unko (Uncle)?

Listen to the first vowel sound! Unko meaning poop starts with an "oo" sound like "moon" or "spoon" (OON-koh). Unko meaning Uncle starts with a short "uh" sound just like English "uncle" (UN-koh). In writing, Uncle is usually capitalized ("Eh, Unko!") or spelled Unco / Uncle to avoid funny mix-ups.

Where did the Hawaiian Pidgin word unko (for poop) come from?

Unko (OON-koh) was borrowed directly from the Japanese word うんこ (unko), a common, family-friendly Japanese word for poop. During Hawaii's plantation era, Japanese vocabulary blended into Hawaiian Pidgin alongside Hawaiian, Portuguese, Cantonese, and Filipino words, so families of all backgrounds in Hawaii grew up saying unko.

Is unko considered a bad or vulgar swear word in Hawaii?

No! Just like the English word "poop," unko (OON-koh) is a mild, everyday family word used by parents, keiki (kids), dog owners, and aunties alike — not a harsh profanity. Locals also use the Hawaiian word kūkae (koo-KAH-eh) with a similar everyday meaning.
