Pidgin Bible FAQ

What is Da Jesus Book?

Da Jesus Book is the New Testament translated into Hawaiian Pidgin (Hawaiʻi Creole English), published in 2000 by Wycliffe Bible Translators. Pidgin speakers raised in Hawaiʻi worked with linguists on it for over a decade.

What is Da Good An Spesho Book?

Da Good An Spesho Book is the complete Bible in Hawaiian Pidgin, Old and New Testaments, published in 2020 by Hawaii Pidgin Bible Ministry. Its New Testament is a revised version of Da Jesus Book, so some verses read differently from the 2000 edition.

Is there a free PDF of the Hawaiian Pidgin Bible?

Yes. eBible.org offers Da Good An Spesho Book as a free PDF of the whole Bible, along with ePub, Kindle and HTML versions. Download the PDF →

Where can I read the Pidgin Bible online?

Read it free on Bible.com or in the YouVersion Bible app (version HPB, Hawaii Pidgin Bible), on eBible.org, or on the Hawaii Pidgin Bible Ministry website. Links are in the reading section above.

How does Psalm 23 start in Hawaiian Pidgin?

Psalm 23:1 in Da Good An Spesho Book reads: "Da One In Charge, he take kea me, Jalike da sheep guy Take kea his sheeps. Az why I get eryting I need." The King James Version has "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." Read all of Psalm 23 in Pidgin →

About Da Jesus Book Translation

Wycliffe Bible Translators

Da Jesus Book was translated by Wycliffe Bible Translators, an international organization dedicated to making the Bible accessible in every language. The Hawaii Pidgin translation project took over a decade and involved:

  • Native Pidgin speakers who grew up with the language
  • Biblical scholars ensuring theological accuracy
  • Linguists who understand Hawaiian Creole English structure
  • Community reviewers from across all Hawaiian islands

Translation Timeline

  • 2000: Da Jesus Book (New Testament) first published
  • 2020: Da Good An Spesho Book (complete Bible with Old Testament) published

Why This Matters

For decades, Hawaiian Pidgin was dismissed as "broken English" and discouraged in schools. Da Jesus Book validates Pidgin as a legitimate language worthy of carrying sacred scripture, affirming the cultural identity of hundreds of thousands of local people.
